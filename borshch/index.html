The whole pot

Ukrainian national dish

Borshchборщ

A sour, beet-red soup cooked in a big pot for the whole family. Every household has its own version, but the structure is the same: a rich broth, a lot of vegetables, a sour note, and a spoon of smetana on top. In English it is often spelled borscht or borsch.

The name most likely comes from borshchivnyk, hogweed, a sour wild plant that went into the pot long before beets did. In 2022 UNESCO added the culture of Ukrainian borshch cooking to its list of intangible heritage in need of urgent safeguarding.

01 · The base

Brothбульйон

Everything starts with meat on the bone, usually pork ribs or beef brisket, covered with cold water and brought slowly to a simmer.

The foam is skimmed off so the broth stays clear. A bay leaf, peppercorns and a whole onion often go in early. For the Lenten version, the broth is made from dried mushrooms and beans instead.

1½–2 hgentle simmerskim often

02 · The body

Potatoesкартопля

Diced potatoes go into the strained broth first, because they need the longest to cook.

They soften the sharpness of the finished soup and make it filling. Some cooks mash one potato against the side of the pot to thicken the broth slightly.

2 cm cubes10–15 min

03 · The heart

Beetsбуряк

Beets give borshch its colour and its earthy sweetness. They are cut into thin strips or coarsely grated and stewed separately, not boiled in the broth.

A splash of vinegar or lemon juice while they stew keeps the colour a deep ruby. Without acid and with too much heat, the red pigment fades to brown.

julienne or grated+ acidstew 15–20 min

04 · The flavour base

Carrot & onionзасмажка

Zasmazhka is the sauté of chopped onion and grated carrot, cooked slowly in sunflower oil or rendered salo until soft and golden.

It adds sweetness and a rounded, toasty depth. Many cooks combine it with the stewed beets in one pan before it all goes into the pot.

sunflower oil or salolow heat8–10 min

05 · The sour note

Tomatoтомат

Tomato paste or grated fresh tomatoes are stirred into the sauté and cooked down for a few minutes.

Together with the acid in the beets, this gives borshch the sourness that defines it. A good borshch balances sour, sweet and savoury, and none of the three should dominate.

2–3 tbsp pasteor 2–3 ripe tomatoes

06 · The texture

Cabbageкапуста

Finely shredded white cabbage goes in near the end, so it keeps a slight bite.

In winter some families use sauerkraut instead of fresh cabbage, which makes the soup more sour. Beans are another common addition at this stage.

thin shreds5–10 minfresh or sauerkraut

07 · The finish

Garlic & greensчасник і зелень

Crushed garlic, chopped dill and parsley are added off the heat. Some cooks pound the garlic with salo into a paste first.

Then the lid goes on and the pot rests for 20–30 minutes. Ukrainians say the borshch has to nastoiatysia, to steep. It is often better the next day.

off the heatrest 20–30 min

08 · In the bowl

Smetana & dillсметана

Each bowl gets a spoonful of smetana, thick sour cream, and a pinch of fresh dill.

On the side: pampushky, soft yeast buns brushed with garlic and oil, or dark rye bread with a thin slice of salo and a clove of raw garlic.

1 heaped spoonpampushkyrye + salo

One dish, many borshches

The basics stay the same, but every region adjusts them.

  • PoltavaMade with poultry, often goose, and served with small wheat dumplings, halushky.
  • LentenNo meat. Broth from dried mushrooms and beans, sometimes with prunes for a smoky sweetness.
  • GreenA spring version made with sorrel instead of beets, finished with a halved boiled egg.
  • ColdKholodnyk: a summer soup of beets, cucumber, radish and dill in a chilled beet broth with kefir or sour cream.
